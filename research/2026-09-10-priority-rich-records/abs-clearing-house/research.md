## Identity and scope corrections

Fresh canonical searches for ABS, CBD, Nagoya, DECLARE, Defra, MoEFCC and the full convention name found no corresponding identity. GETs for both `abs-clearing-house` and `absch` returned 404, so there are no stored incident edges to preserve or repair. GETs confirm `eur` is **European Union / organization**, `jpn` **Japan / country**, and `deu` **Germany / country**. Their existing content and incident edges are left untouched. Full reads and search responses are retained in the canonical JSON files; GET DTOs were not reused as PUT payloads.

The backlog is an unverified lead, not authority. ABSCH is the Nagoya Protocol information mechanism established under [Article 14](https://www.cbd.int/abs/text/articles/default.shtml?sec=abs-14). Its national entries include measures, authorities, permits/IRCCs, checkpoints/CPCs and reports; stakeholders can also submit reference resources. It does not store only government submissions. [Official common forms](https://absch.cbd.int/commonformats) identify both national and reference record families.

An IRCC results when the relevant permit information is made available through ABSCH. Its evidentiary meaning is prior informed consent and mutually agreed terms under the providing Party’s applicable rules, not blanket certification of every later use or benefit-sharing payment. [Article 17](https://www.cbd.int/abs/text/articles/default.shtml?sec=abs-17) controls that distinction. The treaty scope follows [Article 3](https://www.cbd.int/abs/text/articles?sec=abs-03), referring to CBD Article 15 genetic resources and associated traditional knowledge. Do not assert BBNJ ownership/integration, an ABSCH certificate for all ABNJ resources, or that every reference document has been geographically audited. The platform is distinct from BBNJ, and the live South African example disproves a blanket “nothing marine” claim.

Confidentiality correction: public entries may mark some fields confidential, but confidential **values are not to be submitted for publication**. [NP-1/2 annex B.4(d)](https://www.cbd.int/decision/cop/default.shtml?id=13402) is explicit. The [2016 advisory report, paragraphs 42–43](https://www.cbd.int/doc/meetings/abs/np-mop-02/information/np-mop-02-inf-03-en.pdf) explains the implemented distinction between confidential-field indicators and hosted information. The original is retained when an IRCC is amended, with status/version context. Presence of a CPC is not proof of subsequent enforcement or bilateral compliance.

## Measured holdings and access

The following native schema counts were verified with `realm_ss:abs AND _state_s:public AND _latest_s:true`, `rows=0`, and each named `schema_s`. All responses returned `numFoundExact: true` and no unsupported parameters. Exact query URLs, timestamps and replies are in `verified-public-api.json` and are owner-local metric sources in the draft.

| `absCheckpointCommunique` | 422 | Checkpoint information records, not additional permits |

Permit expiry was not filtered: current published versions can describe expired permits. These are separate subsets, not a claimed total of all ABSCH holdings, biological samples, sequences, permits issued nationally or Parties. `record_count` is appropriate for these native catalogue entries. No storage-size figure was verified. The current [NP-5/4 decision](https://www.cbd.int/doc/decisions/np-mop-05/np-mop-05-dec-04-en.pdf), paragraph 4, expressly notes incomplete national publication.

Usage research found approximately 975 weekly visitors in 2023 and approximately 14,000 new visitors during 1 July–1 December 2023 in the [report prepared for the February 2024 advisory meeting](https://www.cbd.int/doc/c/e1c2/4b9c/68bbb45c8d34f6cacf2b9f03/np-absch-iac-2024-01-02-en.pdf), paragraphs 9–13. These are visitors, not approved `session_count`; no conversion was invented. Analytics definitions changed on 1 July 2023. The report covers December 2022–December 2023, despite its apparent cover-year typo “16 January 2023”; it is not a 2026 usage measurement. No current approved download/contributor/citation metric was verified.

The [current developer hub](https://developer.cbd.int/) and [IRCC search documentation](https://developer.cbd.int/clearing-house/absch/ircc/solr.html) document public JSON search and individual document retrieval. Actual document reads succeeded for the South African IRCC and UK CPC using their case-sensitive document identifiers. A first exploratory query used the wrong field and later uppercase values: those zero results are preserved in `public-api-probes.json`, corrected with the actual lowercase `uniqueIdentifier_s` values, and never treated as absence or metric evidence.

The portal renders actual permit/checkpoint content in HTML. A real [library PDF attachment](https://absch.cbd.int/api/v2013/documents/3B50BCD4-C829-5953-9528-6FA9375176BB/attachments/An%20Explanatory%20Guide%20to%20the%20Nagoya%20Protocol%20on%20Access%20and%20Benefit-sharing%20%28English%29.pdf) returned HTTP 200, establishing PDF for retrievable reference content, not the underlying database format. CSV/XLSX export leads and UI export templates were seen, but no complete export was tested; these formats were not assigned. Offline Word forms are described in prose because no approved Word format ID exists. Types remain catalogue records and documents; no sequence/sample type is inferred from a permit’s scientific subject. `law` is the only discipline tag.

Public reading is free and unauthenticated; this is separate from reuse rights. The linked [CBD terms](https://www.cbd.int/terms) give personal noncommercial copying permission subject to more specific restrictions and do not give general resale, redistribution or derivative-work rights. No CC licence was inferred. Native data or document-specific rights still require inspection.

[National submission](https://absch.cbd.int/about/accounts) needs a CBD account and official PA/NAU designation. NAUs draft; PAs authorize publication. [Stakeholder reference submission](https://absch.cbd.int/kb/tags/getting-started/Getting-started-using-the-ABS-Clearing-House-for-Stakeholders/5bbe3a4d64f00600011e20d9) is open to registered users and receives Secretariat validation; this routine curation is described in prose, not coded as an extra participation-approval requirement. The national API requires an access token and government-scoped authority. No authenticated mutation, secret field access, notification delivery, bulk export, quota or fee schedule was tested. The offline national mechanism is mediated by the Secretariat. Ryu machine routes remain empty pending an operational contract/approval; API access evidence is retained in the normal access section.

ABSCH common forms, controlled vocabularies and native schemas were investigated. None has an approved Ryu standard ID, and no implementation of another approved standard was verified. Legal instruments, IRCC IDs and JSON do not justify Darwin Core, Dublin Core, DCAT or other standard assignments. This is a specific standards gap in every locale.
