OceanTeacher Global Academy / IOC CHM-TMT

Why:  Article 51(3)(b) makes the Clearing-House responsible for matching stated needs with available opportunities. That's a live function the CHM has to perform, not a document library. OceanTeacher is the strongest existing anchor. It's IOC-UNESCO's global training network, run out of the IODE project office in Ostend through seventeen regional and specialized training centres, with over 250 courses delivered to more than 15,000 learners from 130 Member States between 2015 and 2025, taught in English, Spanish, French and Portuguese, all openly licensed. That's the actual delivery machinery for marine technology transfer, and it's already distributed through the regions rather than centralized, which is the model Part V implies. The second reason is lineage. The IOC's Clearing-House Mechanism for the Transfer of Marine Technology is the direct ancestor of the BBNJ CHM. The 2020 IOC proposal that created Ocean InfoHub was titled, in full, a proposal to develop an IOC clearing-house mechanism for transfer of marine technology powered by a proof-of-concept ODIS architecture. So the CHM-TMT is why ODIS exists, and ODIS is what the BBNJ CHM prototype is expected to build on. Ryu is being built on top of a lineage that runs directly through this record. Including it makes that visible.

Narrative
 OceanTeacher is IOC-UNESCO's capacity development platform, run from the IOC Project Office for IODE in Ostend, Belgium, and delivered through seventeen regional and specialized training centres rather than from a single location. Courses cover ocean science, ocean services and marine data management, and are offered both as facilitated cohorts and as open self-study. Between 2015 and 2025 the network delivered over 250 courses to more than 15,000 learners from 130 IOC Member States, teaching in English, Spanish, French and Portuguese, with all content released under Creative Commons licences. The distributed structure is the point: a trainee in the Pacific or West Africa works through a regional centre in their own region and often their own language, rather than travelling or applying to a central institution. OceanTeacher sits inside a wider IOC apparatus for technology transfer that traces back to the 2005 IOC Criteria and Guidelines on the Transfer of Marine Technology, and connects to OceanExpert, IOC's directory of marine experts and institutions, which is itself an ODIS node. The lineage matters for anyone building on ODIS: the 2020 IOC proposal that created Ocean InfoHub was explicitly framed as developing an IOC clearing-house mechanism for transfer of marine technology powered by a proof-of-concept ODIS architecture, making this the conceptual ancestor of the BBNJ Clearing-House. Two caveats belong on the record. This is a training and knowledge platform, not a scientific dataset, so its holdings are courses, experts and learning materials rather than observations or sequences. And participant-level data are not published, for privacy reasons, so usage figures are reported in aggregate by the programme rather than queryable.
