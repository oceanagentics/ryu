## Access and standards findings

The public [REST response for Solea solea](https://www.marinespecies.org/rest/AphiaRecordByAphiaID/127160) returned JSON without authentication and confirmed accepted status, scientific authority, classification and a FishBase citation. [Service documentation](https://www.marinespecies.org/aphia.php?p=webservice) supports REST/SOAP, warns against complete API harvesting and documents RDF/XML and the Aphia event stream. The live [LDES feed](https://aphia.org/feed) returned Turtle containing actual Dublin Core `modified`/`isVersionOf` terms. RDF serializations without approved format IDs are described in guidance, not added as new vocabulary. Darwin Core is scoped to approved taxonomic CSV export; EML to type-locality dataset metadata; Dublin Core to observed LDES metadata. No inference of CF, identifiers-as-standards or licenses-as-standards.

The [request page](https://www.marinespecies.org/usersrequest.php) now requires an institutional-email account/login, stated intended use and approval. The provided CSV covers taxonomy only, excludes AlgaeBase content and is supplied quarterly under the stated agreement. The About page also mentions monthly archiving; archiving and contractual bulk delivery cadence are kept distinct. The [GBIF registry API](https://api.gbif.org/v1/dataset/2d59e5db-57ad-41ff-97d6-11f5fb264527) reports a 1 September 2026 checklist publication, newer than the cached 1 August web page. Its archive endpoint is explicitly labelled **Restricted to GBIF** and the archive returned 403. The backlog's unrestricted full-archive download claim is corrected. The public type-locality archive remains usable.

[Contributions](https://www.marinespecies.org/contribute.php) go through DMT feedback or appointed editor accounts; no unauthenticated write API is claimed. Public browsing, taxon matching, R-client and services are represented separately where useful. Browser CORS support is not promised; the single server-side probe did not establish browser compatibility. No approved Ryu runtime route exists, so `routes` remains empty.

## All six relationship types reviewed

- **Governs:** retain `worms-steering-committee → worms`. The [2015 terms of reference](https://www.marinespecies.org/documents/MoUs_ToUs/WoRMS_Terms_of_Reference.pdf) expressly assign scientific correctness, editor appointment/replacement and download licensing. The current About page confirms the committee's ongoing role. Do not infer governance by editors' individual employer institutions, Belgium, funders or the hosting infrastructure.
- **Operates:** retain `flanders-marine-institute → worms`. VLIZ appoints the DMT, maintains the database and identifiers, supports editors and supplies approved exports. SC policy authority is distinct from operation.
- **Funds:** propose `lifewatch-belgium → worms`, through VLIZ, for DMT support. The [April 2025 committee report](https://marinespecies.org/documents/Steering%20Committee%20reports/20250422_WoRMS_SC_meeting_Ostend_report.pdf) confirms transition support for 2025–2026. Regular VLIZ subsidies from 2027 were an intended change, not an established grant. Historical EC projects, Sloan/CoML, GBIF, DFO, MEDIN and NSF CORONA were examined on the funding page: they are specific historical leads, but additional edges are withheld pending recipient/intermediary and grant-period verification, rather than converting the undated service-grant examples into current funding.
- **Member of:** examined Aphia, LifeWatch Species Information Backbone, Marine Life 2030, ABC WoRMS/Ocean Decade, Challenger 150 and the subregisters (MolluscaBase, WoRDSS, WRiMS, ERMS and CCZ). [LifeWatch's own description](https://www.lifewatch.be/biodiversity-data-systems) establishes shared components, not membership. Subregister contexts are database architecture. Ocean Decade endorsement and project partnerships do not establish an eligible system-to-system membership; no membership edge asserted.
- **Publishes to:** propose `worms-editorial-board → worms`. The terms and current About page establish the active editors/data providers' online and DMT-mediated contribution workflow. Individual institutional affiliations do not establish organizational submissions. The missing collective endpoint is drafted. Prospective Ocean Census species discovery and ISA/Sustainable Seabed Knowledge contributions remain collaboration leads; no completed specific payload transfer was verified here.
- **Syncs to:** retain `fishbase → worms` and propose the other eight named external imports: AlgaeBase, SeaLifeBase, Index Fungorum, ICTV taxonomy database, Claudia Mills's Ctenophora name list, Reptile Database, FADA, and Recent & Fossil Bryozoa. The [current external-partner list](https://marinespecies.org/about.php#partners) explicitly describes content pulled into Aphia; each edge states chiefly taxonomy, unspecified method/cadence/latest import, and no reciprocal inference. Provider pages were followed where accessible; AlgaeBase/SeaLifeBase web requests were blocked, FADA retrieval failed. This does not erase the receiving system's documented import relationship, but operational access is not certified.

Outgoing transfers are separately evidenced: `worms → platform-obis` is taxonomic enrichment during ingestion ([OBIS explanation](https://obis.org/sources/worms/), [download field guidance](https://manual.obis.org/access.html)); `worms → eurobis` covers the released type-locality archive and shared taxonomy enrichment; `worms → emodnet-biology` covers the explicitly documented [shared publication pipeline](https://emodnet.ec.europa.eu/en/biology); `worms → gbif` is the registered restricted checklist feed; `worms → catalogue-of-life` is a [COL-maintained import entry](https://github.com/CatalogueOfLife/testing/issues/299) recording synchronization on 3 January 2026. The COL entry's source version says 2025-01-01 and is preserved verbatim as an anomaly, not silently corrected or treated as the latest current snapshot. GenBank deep links do not show sequence transfer. Goals to strengthen BoLD/GOOS connections do not prove active data delivery. No reverse OBIS/WoRMS sync is inferred from joint quality review.

`obis-edge.json` is the agreed shared taxonomy edge for the OBIS worker. Root should reconcile shared edges once, with fresh reads. Endpoint proposals in `endpoint-drafts.json`: LifeWatch Belgium (organization), Catalogue of Life (system), WoRMS Editorial Board (organization), and six missing external provider systems. They are English identity stubs with six-language source titles, not rich records or production creations. FADA's URL remains a verification caveat. No arbitrary system nesting was added.
