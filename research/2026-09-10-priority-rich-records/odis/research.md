## Identity and primary findings

The [ODIS overview](https://odis.org/overview/) and [architecture documentation](https://book.odis.org/) establish an interoperability federation of independently managed ocean information systems. ODISCat is its source-registration catalogue; the development search indexes descriptions of resources supplied by participating systems. Providers retain their original holdings, licences and stewardship. A source entry, harvested metadata record and underlying dataset are different objects.

The backlog conflated ODIS, its catalogue and Ocean InfoHub. The [current ODIS homepage](https://odis.org/) says the Global Search Hub is being redeveloped and links a working demo. The [Ocean InfoHub notice](https://oceaninfohub.org/) reports project closure in June 2024 and says its old search interface is no longer maintained. [FUST's account](https://fust.iode.org/en/oih) separately identifies an April 2020–April 2024 funding interval. Neither makes the retired interface the current federation. The backlog's assertion that Ryu already conforms to ODIS is not demonstrated by this research; any Ryu mapping and conformance validation is separate implementation work.

## Access and standards

The [catalogue](https://catalogue.odis.org/), demo and sampled JSON record API are publicly readable without accounts or keys. Provider metadata feeds remain provider-specific: blanket free access, unrestricted reuse or anonymous availability for every endpoint is not established. The catalogue's [licence footer and disclaimer](https://catalogue.odis.org/disclaimer) link CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 without relicensing linked holdings.

The [joining guide](https://book.odis.org/gettingStarted.html) describes an initially approved OceanExpert account, an organization-sanctioned identity, source registration, and publication of Schema.org JSON-LD through a sitemap or sitegraph. IODE provides light curation. The catalogue separately offers an email suggestion route without OceanExpert login; no suggestion was sent. Contribution charges are unspecified. Registration enables discovery but is not proof of indexing.

The candidate assigns the approved `schema_org` standard with scoped six-language evidence. JSON, XML and HTML describe actual metadata delivery surfaces. [SHACL guidance](https://book.odis.org/validation/index.html) describes initial validation work; it does not establish mandatory, system-wide SHACL certification. DCAT/ISO conformance, OceanExpert as a separate standard, and standards inherited from individual nodes remain unassigned.

`machine-probes.json` records HTTPS hostname-verification failures for the documented dashboard and graph host, plus HTTP 404 for the documented SPARQL request. Dashboard Parquet exports were not verified. The working development API has no verified stability guarantee. `routes` remains empty: this research does not approve an operational Ryu route.
